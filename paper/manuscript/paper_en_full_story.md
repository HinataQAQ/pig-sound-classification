# Pig Vocalization Recognition under Simulated Environmental Noise Using Duration-Aware Hierarchical Representations and Post-hoc Prototype Inference

## Abstract

Automatic pig vocalization recognition is a practical sensing problem for livestock monitoring, but its reliability depends on more than selecting a stronger acoustic frontend or a larger classifier. Pig vocal events often contain short temporal build-up, sustained acoustic texture, and post-event context; therefore, overly short clips may truncate useful cues, whereas overly long clips may admit irrelevant background. In this study, we restructure the closed-set pig sound recognition pipeline around three evidence-driven decisions: duration-aware context selection, hierarchical auxiliary supervision, and post-hoc prototype inference. The task covers four main classes, cough, calm_grunt, feeding and stress_vocal, with six auxiliary subtypes used only for hierarchical supervision. Clean experiments used class-capped cap3x training expansion, Log-Mel features and a convolutional recurrent neural network (CRNN) backbone across 5 folds x 5 seeds. The decisive clean-data gain came from 2 s context: Macro-F1 increased from 0.9226 for 1 s Log-Mel to 0.9472 for 2 s Log-Mel, with a paired mean delta of 0.0246 and Wilcoxon p=0.000162. More complex clean-audio modifications, including MCTAFD variants, PCEN, spectral-gate denoising, SpecAugment and attention pooling, did not provide a more stable mainline in the locked result package. Hierarchical auxiliary supervision increased the clean mean Macro-F1 up to 0.9515 at lambda=1.0, while lambda=0.5 gave the lowest cross-run standard deviation among the tested hierarchical settings; however, its gain over the 2 s baseline was not statistically significant and is therefore interpreted as a fine-grained semantic and boundary-analysis contribution rather than as a strong performance claim. Post-hoc prototypes were then evaluated without noisy retraining. Clean hierarchical prototypes reached the highest Macro-F1 among the prototype variants (0.9540), but under DEMAND simulated additive noise the main-class prototype was the most stable: under ALL_NOISY conditions it achieved Macro-F1 0.6230 compared with 0.6173 for raw Softmax, with mean delta 0.005755, 95% bootstrap CI [0.001606, 0.010894] and Wilcoxon p=0.010511. These results support main-class prototype inference as a modest but statistically supported simulated-noise robustness mechanism. The study remains bounded: DEMAND is simulated additive noise rather than real-farm external validation, 0 dB active-event SNR is an extreme stress condition rather than a no-noise setting, cough recognition at 0 dB is unreliable, prototypes do not provide a universal uncertainty improvement, and hierarchical prototypes are weaker than main prototypes under noise.

## Keywords

Pig vocalization recognition; Log-Mel; CRNN; hierarchical supervision; acoustic prototype; simulated additive noise; DEMAND; selective prediction

## Introduction

Acoustic monitoring can provide a non-contact route for observing animal health and behavior in intensive pig production. Pig sounds are not only isolated impulses: cough, calm grunting, feeding-related sounds and stress vocalizations can include onset transients, repeated texture and short contextual patterns. A recognition system that treats each event as a single instantaneous frame may therefore discard useful temporal evidence, while a system that extends context indiscriminately may absorb irrelevant environmental background. The engineering question is not only which model is expressive enough, but also what event window gives the model a fair representation of the vocalization.

The current result package shows why this question matters. A 1 s Log-Mel CRNN baseline achieved Macro-F1 0.9226, while the 2 s Log-Mel CRNN achieved 0.9472 across 25 fold-seed runs. The paired duration comparison gave a mean Macro-F1 delta of 0.0246 and Wilcoxon p=0.000162. A 3 s Log-Mel setting reached 0.9434 in the available 15-run comparison, which did not surpass the 2 s mainline. The same ablation package did not support a return to feature stacking as the primary direction: tabled MCTAFD, PCEN, spectral-gate denoising, SpecAugment and attention-pooling variants all remained below the locked 2 s Log-Mel CRNN mean. The project record also retains BiLSTM replacement as a completed negative direction rather than as the validated clean-audio mainline. These observations narrow the first contribution: the clean-data advance is an evidence-based shift from adding components to identifying a suitable event context.

The second challenge is semantic granularity. The four main labels are useful for deployment-facing reporting, but they compress acoustically meaningful distinctions. Cough contains dry_cough and abdominal_cough; stress_vocal contains frightened_stress and anxious_stress; calm_grunt and feeding also define subtype targets. The feeding/stress_vocal boundary remained an important confusion direction in the result package. Hierarchical auxiliary supervision addresses this by binding the four-class main task to a six-subtype auxiliary task through a loss of the form main loss + lambda x auxiliary loss. This design asks the representation to preserve subtype-level acoustic structure without replacing the main task. The locked results require careful wording: lambda=1.0 achieved the highest mean Macro-F1 among the tested auxiliary weights, whereas lambda=0.5 gave the lowest standard deviation and is the balanced setting. The incremental hierarchical gain over the 2 s baseline was not statistically significant, so the contribution is interpretability and boundary shaping rather than a claim of decisive performance improvement.

The third challenge is robustness under environmental interference. Raw Softmax inference represents discriminative decision boundaries learned under clean training conditions. Additive noise may shift embeddings toward regions where those boundaries are less reliable. Post-hoc prototype inference provides a different frozen-test decision rule: train-split embeddings define class centers, validation data calibrate temperatures and thresholds, and test data are used only for evaluation. This study evaluates two prototype forms. The main prototype uses only the four main-class centers. The hierarchical prototype also incorporates the six subtype prototypes. Clean results favor the hierarchical prototype, consistent with its semantic role, but simulated DEMAND noise favors the simpler main-class prototype. This difference is central to the paper's story: fine-grained prototypes are useful for clean semantic explanation, whereas main-class prototypes are better suited to the medium-noise robustness setting evaluated here.

This paper therefore makes three bounded contributions. First, it identifies 2 s Log-Mel CRNN as the clean closed-set mainline through matched fold-seed evidence, rather than through an untested preference for longer context. Second, it uses hierarchical auxiliary supervision to inject subtype semantics into the representation while explicitly avoiding a significance claim that the data do not support. Third, it evaluates post-hoc main and hierarchical prototypes under a frozen DEMAND simulated-noise protocol and shows that main-class prototypes provide a small but statistically supported aggregate Macro-F1 gain under simulated additive noise. The scope is deliberately limited: the study does not report real-farm external validation, unknown-class rejection, noise-aware retraining, or reliable cough recognition at 0 dB active-event SNR.

## Related Work

Automatic animal-sound recognition combines bioacoustic sensing with supervised audio classification. Prior work has used pig-vocalization analysis to characterize call valence and production context, to support animal-welfare monitoring, and to study pig-cough or sick-cough recognition [@briefer2022pigcalls; @mcloughlin2019automated; @coutant2024scoping; @ferrari2008cough; @exadaktylos2008cough; @yin2021pigcoughcnn; @shen2022pigcoughfusion]. Recent smart-farm studies also address abnormal pig vocalizations and noisy farm-environment classification [@xie2024abnormalpig; @chung2025pvmc]. The present manuscript should not claim originality by ignoring that literature. Instead, its distinction should be framed around the evidence-controlled engineering sequence: duration selection, hierarchical subtype supervision and frozen prototype inference under simulated additive noise.

CRNN architectures have been widely used for audio classification because convolutional layers can extract local time-frequency patterns while recurrent layers summarize temporal dependencies [@choi2017crnn]. In this work, the CRNN is not presented as a new architecture. Its role is to provide a stable backbone for testing which input duration and supervision structure are actually supported by the pig-vocalization evidence. Log-Mel features were extracted using standard audio-analysis tooling [@mcfee2015librosa], and the clean ablations showed that increasing frontend complexity did not outperform the 2 s Log-Mel mainline.

Prototype-based inference is related to prototypical networks, where class representations are expressed as centers in an embedding space [@snell2017prototypical]. This paper does not claim to introduce prototype learning. The prototypes here are post-hoc, fold-seed-specific artifacts built from frozen clean train embeddings, calibrated on validation data and evaluated on held-out test predictions. This distinction matters because the contribution is not a new trainable prototype architecture; it is a leakage-controlled inference layer that asks whether clean embedding centers can stabilize decisions under simulated additive noise.

Selective prediction and risk-coverage analysis provide a way to evaluate whether confidence or distance scores can identify unreliable predictions [@geifman2017selective]. The corrected AUGRC calculations in this paper follow the generalized-risk convention used by the fd-shifts implementation [@fdshifts_riskcoverage]. The results do not support a broad claim that prototypes improve uncertainty ranking: under ALL_NOISY conditions, raw Softmax had lower AURC/AUGRC than prototype variants. Prototype benefits are therefore limited to aggregate Macro-F1 and frozen-threshold selective-risk behavior, not universal uncertainty calibration.

## Materials and Methods

### Task definition and label structure

The task is closed-set classification of pig vocal events into four main classes: cough, calm_grunt, feeding and stress_vocal. A six-subtype auxiliary label space was used for hierarchical supervision: dry_cough, abdominal_cough, calm_grunt, feeding, frightened_stress and anxious_stress. The auxiliary task does not change the deployment-facing main label set. Instead, it provides finer acoustic constraints during representation learning and later enables hierarchical prototype analysis.

### Clean mainline model

The clean mainline uses class-capped cap3x training expansion, 2 s Log-Mel input features and a CRNN backbone. The duration choice was treated as an experimental variable rather than a fixed assumption. The 1 s condition tests whether a compact event slice is sufficient; the 2 s condition tests whether short context improves recognition; the 3 s condition tests whether adding more context continues to help or begins to include redundant background. All clean comparisons were interpreted through matched fold-seed evidence where available.

### Hierarchical auxiliary supervision

Hierarchical supervision adds a subtype prediction head to the main-class classifier. The training objective is:

`L = L_main + lambda * L_aux`.

The main loss optimizes the four-class decision task, while the auxiliary loss encourages the embedding to preserve subtype structure. Three auxiliary weights were evaluated in the locked table: lambda=0.2, lambda=0.5 and lambda=1.0. The manuscript uses lambda=0.5 as the balanced setting because it has the lowest standard deviation across runs, while reporting that lambda=1.0 has the highest mean Macro-F1. This separation prevents the paper from turning a small nonsignificant mean improvement into an overstated performance claim.

### Post-hoc prototype inference

Prototype inference was applied after training. For each fold-seed run, train-split embeddings were used to compute class centers. The main prototype method uses four centers, one per main class. The hierarchical prototype method additionally uses six subtype centers. Validation predictions were used to calibrate temperatures, thresholds and fusion parameters where relevant. The frozen test split was used only for evaluation. No prototype, threshold or calibration artifact was constructed by mixing folds or seeds.

The raw Softmax decision rule and prototype decision rule answer different questions. Raw Softmax evaluates the discriminative boundary learned by the classifier. Prototype inference evaluates whether a test embedding remains close to class-level acoustic centers learned from the training split. Under clean conditions, subtype prototypes can support semantic structure. Under additive noise, however, subtype-level centers may be more fragile than coarser main-class centers.

### Simulated additive-noise protocol

Noise robustness was evaluated with DEMAND environmental recordings [@demand2018]. The selected environments were DWASHING, TBUS and STRAFFIC. The protocol mixed deterministic DEMAND segments into the 2 s pig-vocalization waveform at active-event SNRs of 20, 10 and 0 dB. The 0 dB condition is described throughout as an extreme active-event SNR stress condition, not as no noise and not as an ordinary deployment scenario.

The noisy evaluation was zero-shot and frozen. It reused the clean checkpoint, train-only clean prototypes, validation-only clean calibration, frozen rejection thresholds and frozen fusion parameters. No noisy validation recalibration or noisy test tuning was allowed. The deterministic noise selection key used SHA256 and included the fold, normalized clean path, clean MD5, DEMAND environment identity, noise-file SHA256, global noise seed and repeat. It deliberately excluded model seed and SNR so that a fixed clean sample and environment reused the same noise segment across seeds and SNR levels, with only gain changing across SNR.

## Results

### Story 1: 2 s context, not feature stacking, defines the clean mainline

The first question was whether pig vocal events could be represented reliably by a short 1 s input. The 1 s Log-Mel CRNN reached mean Macro-F1 0.9226 across 25 runs. This was a reasonable baseline, but it exposed a practical limitation: feeding and stress_vocal events are not always captured by a single isolated instant, and cough-related context can also be short but temporally structured. The next attempt was to enrich the frontend and architecture. The locked ablation table includes MCTAFD variants, PCEN, spectral-gate denoising, SpecAugment and attention pooling. None provided a more stable clean mainline than 2 s Log-Mel CRNN: cap3x logmel_dur2 reached 0.9472, compared with 0.9208 for cap3x mctafd_no_se_gated, 0.8916 for logmel_pcen, 0.9093 for logmel_sg, 0.9198 for logmel_specaug and 0.9431 for logmel_dur2_attn in the available locked comparisons.

This failure evidence changed the design logic. The project did not continue to stack more handcrafted or augmented features as the primary clean-audio path. Instead, it treated event duration as the first-order design variable. The 2 s Log-Mel CRNN improved over the 1 s Log-Mel baseline by a paired mean Macro-F1 delta of 0.0246, with Wilcoxon p=0.000162. A 3 s Log-Mel model reached 0.9434 in the available 15-run comparison, below the 2 s mean. The final clean mechanism is therefore duration-aware but restrained: the model is given enough local acoustic context to represent pig vocal events, without expanding to a longer window that may admit redundant background.

The boundary of this result is also clear. The data support 2 s as the validated clean mainline for this closed-set protocol, not as a universal duration for all pig-acoustic tasks. The 3 s comparison has fewer runs than the 2 s and 1 s paired comparison, and the result should be interpreted as evidence that 2 s is the best supported setting in this package, not as a proof that longer windows can never help.

### Story 2: hierarchical supervision adds subtype semantics, not a statistically significant main-performance claim

The second question was whether four main labels were too coarse for representation learning. The main label cough groups dry_cough and abdominal_cough; stress_vocal groups frightened_stress and anxious_stress. Feeding and stress_vocal also form a difficult boundary in the confusion summaries. A pure four-class objective can learn a deployment-facing classifier, but it does not explicitly encode these finer acoustic distinctions.

The attempted mechanism was hierarchical auxiliary supervision. The main task remained the four-class classification problem, while a subtype head predicted six auxiliary labels. The loss combined the two objectives as main loss + lambda x auxiliary loss. In the locked auxiliary-weight table, lambda=0.2, lambda=0.5 and lambda=1.0 gave mean Macro-F1 values of 0.9505, 0.9510 and 0.9515, respectively. Lambda=1.0 therefore had the highest mean. Lambda=0.5 had the lowest cross-run standard deviation, 0.0124, and is the balanced setting used for the prototype and noise studies.

The failure evidence is important. The hierarchical gain over the 2 s baseline did not reach statistical significance, so the paper should not describe hierarchical supervision as a significant clean-performance improvement. Its role is more specific: it introduces fine-grained acoustic semantics and offers a better way to analyze boundary behavior. The clean prototype summary is consistent with this interpretation. Raw Softmax at lambda=0.5 achieved Macro-F1 0.9510, main prototype achieved 0.9519, and hierarchical prototype achieved 0.9540. However, hierarchical prototype minus raw Softmax had mean delta 0.002937 with 95% CI [-0.000478, 0.007294] and Wilcoxon p=0.058253. This result is close but still nonsignificant under the locked criterion.

The final mechanism is therefore not "hierarchy wins everywhere." It is a representation and interpretation device. Hierarchical labels help organize the acoustic space and support clean semantic analysis, but the manuscript must keep its performance claim bounded.

### Story 3: post-hoc prototypes separate clean semantic explanation from simulated-noise robustness

The third question was how the clean model behaves when environmental noise shifts the input distribution. Raw Softmax is tied to the discriminative boundary learned during clean training. Under simulated additive noise, this boundary can drift because the embedding of a noisy event may move in directions not seen during training. Prototype inference tests a complementary decision rule: whether an embedding remains near train-derived class centers.

Two prototype mechanisms were evaluated. The main prototype uses four main-class centers. The hierarchical prototype adds six subtype centers. Clean results favored the hierarchical prototype, with mean Macro-F1 0.9540 compared with 0.9519 for main prototype and 0.9510 for raw Softmax. This supports the semantic role of subtype prototypes under clean conditions. The noise results, however, reversed the practical preference. Under ALL_NOISY DEMAND simulated additive noise, raw Softmax achieved Macro-F1 0.6173, main prototype achieved 0.6230 and hierarchical prototype achieved 0.6200. Main prototype minus raw Softmax had mean delta 0.005755, 95% bootstrap CI [0.001606, 0.010894] and Wilcoxon p=0.010511. Hierarchical prototype minus raw Softmax had mean delta 0.002710 with p=0.312333, and hierarchical prototype was significantly below main prototype under ALL_NOISY conditions (mean delta -0.003045, p=0.000376).

This evidence defines the final mechanism. The main prototype is more robust under the evaluated simulated noise because it uses coarser class centers that are less sensitive to subtype-level perturbations. The hierarchical prototype remains useful for clean semantic explanation, but under noise it can over-fragment the embedding space. The moderate-noise stratum supports the same direction: main prototype achieved Macro-F1 0.6534 versus 0.6472 for raw Softmax and 0.6522 for hierarchical prototype, with prototype minus raw Softmax p=0.001673. In the extreme-stress stratum, main prototype remained numerically above raw Softmax (0.5624 versus 0.5574), but the paired p-value was 0.560171, so this condition should not be described as a significant robustness gain.

Per-class results expose the main boundary. Under ALL_NOISY conditions, cough F1 remained very low for all methods: 0.0867 for raw Softmax, 0.0974 for main prototype and 0.0957 for hierarchical prototype. Under 0 dB active-event SNR, cough recall was zero for STRAFFIC and TBUS across all methods and below 0.009 in DWASHING. Thus, the 0 dB condition is an extreme stress test and cough recognition there is unreliable. Feeding and stress_vocal also remained entangled, but main prototype improved several aggregate boundary metrics relative to raw Softmax under ALL_NOISY conditions.

Uncertainty results place another boundary on the claim. Prototypes did not comprehensively improve uncertainty or ranking. Under ALL_NOISY conditions, raw Softmax had lower AURC/AUGRC (0.1775/0.1239) than main prototype (0.2109/0.1394) and hierarchical prototype (0.2122/0.1403). Prototype inference showed a slight frozen-threshold selective-risk improvement under ALL_NOISY conditions, with mean frozen-threshold selective risk 0.2988 for main prototype versus 0.3024 for raw Softmax, but this should not be generalized into an uncertainty-calibration claim. The fused variant is retained as an ablation and sanity check, not as the main innovation.

## Discussion

The main lesson is that practical pig-vocalization recognition benefits from a sequence of narrowing decisions. The first narrowing decision is temporal: before adding more complex acoustic frontends, the system needs an event window that captures the relevant vocal context. In this result package, 2 s provided that window. The second narrowing decision is semantic: subtype labels can enrich the representation, but their contribution must be framed as semantic and boundary-oriented because the main-performance gain over the 2 s baseline was not statistically significant. The third narrowing decision is inferential: clean subtype prototypes support semantic explanation, whereas main-class prototypes are better suited to simulated-noise robustness.

This interpretation also explains why the noise results differ from the clean prototype results. Hierarchical prototypes introduce more detailed acoustic centers. Under clean conditions, this detail can help organize the embedding space. Under additive noise, however, the same detail may become brittle because noise can move samples away from subtype-specific centers even when the main-class identity remains recoverable. The main prototype acts as a coarser attractor and therefore provides the most stable aggregate noise gain in the evaluated DEMAND protocol.

The uncertainty findings prevent a stronger claim. If prototypes had improved Macro-F1, calibration metrics and risk-coverage ranking simultaneously, the method could be framed as a general reliability improvement. The locked tables do not support that. Raw Softmax ranked better by AURC/AUGRC under ALL_NOISY, MODERATE_NOISE and EXTREME_STRESS aggregate rows, while prototype gains appeared mainly in Macro-F1 and frozen-threshold selective risk. The manuscript should therefore describe prototype inference as a robustness-oriented decision rule, not as a universal uncertainty solution.

The simulated-noise protocol is useful but incomplete. DEMAND provides public environmental recordings and deterministic provenance, but additive DEMAND mixing is not the same as real pig-farm external validation. Real barns include reverberation, overlapping animals, sensor placement changes, husbandry equipment and label-distribution shifts that are not captured by this protocol. The 0 dB active-event SNR setting is particularly severe and should be interpreted as stress testing. The cough collapse under 0 dB makes this limitation central rather than incidental.

## Limitations

First, the noise evaluation uses simulated additive DEMAND noise only. It does not establish external validity in real pig farms. Second, the task is closed-set and does not address unknown sounds or open-set rejection. Third, cough recognition under 0 dB active-event SNR is unreliable, with zero cough recall in STRAFFIC and TBUS for all methods in the appendix summary. Fourth, hierarchical auxiliary supervision improves the clean mean but does not significantly outperform the 2 s baseline, and clean hierarchical prototype improvement over raw Softmax remains nonsignificant at p=0.058253. Fifth, prototype inference does not comprehensively improve uncertainty: raw Softmax has better AURC/AUGRC ranking in the aggregate noise summaries. Sixth, the fused method is an ablation and should not be promoted as a core contribution. Finally, target-journal formatting, full animal-ethics provenance, data-sharing permissions for original pig audio and complete livestock-acoustics citations remain to be finalized.

## Conclusion

This study restructures pig-vocalization recognition around three bounded findings. A 2 s Log-Mel CRNN is the best supported clean closed-set mainline in the locked result package, with a significant paired improvement over 1 s input. Hierarchical auxiliary supervision adds subtype semantics and supports boundary analysis, but its incremental clean gain is not statistically significant and should not be overstated. Post-hoc prototype inference separates two roles: hierarchical prototypes are most useful for clean semantic explanation, whereas main-class prototypes provide the most reliable aggregate simulated-noise robustness under the frozen DEMAND protocol. The work offers a reproducible path toward noise-aware pig-vocalization inference, while leaving real-farm validation, robust cough recognition under severe noise and open-set deployment as necessary next steps.
